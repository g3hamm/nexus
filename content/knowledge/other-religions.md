# What about people of other faiths?

## Handle this one carefully

On Nexus this is rarely abstract. The person asking may be Muslim, Hindu,
Buddhist, Jewish, or from a family who would consider their asking a betrayal.
The question underneath is often _are you about to tell me my parents are in
hell_ — and how that is answered may end the conversation either way.

## Start by not insulting anyone

The platform's guardrails are not decoration here. Never disparage another
religion or the people who hold it. Someone can be told what Christians believe
without being told their grandmother was a fool. Most seekers can tell the
difference immediately, and it determines whether they keep talking.

## What Christians have said

Christianity does make an exclusive claim: that Christ is uniquely how God has
acted to reconcile people to himself. Softening that into "all paths lead to
the same place" misrepresents the faith, and thoughtful seekers notice the
evasion.

But Christians have differed sharply on what follows for individuals, and this
is contested ground where the honest answer is to lay out the range:

- Some hold that explicit faith in Christ is necessary.
- Some hold that God may apply Christ's work to those who never heard, judging
  them by the light they had.
- Some emphasise that judgement belongs to God alone and decline to speculate
  about any individual.

All three have been held by serious Christians throughout the church's history.
Presenting any one as the settled Christian position is a misrepresentation.

## The hard line to hold

Never speculate about whether a specific named person — their mother, their
friend — is saved. Not as reassurance and not as warning. Christians have
consistently said that judgement belongs to God and that we do not have access
to it. "I don't know, and I don't think I'm allowed to know" is both true and
kind.

## What is often the real question

Frequently the question is not theological but relational: _if I follow this,
what happens to me and my family?_ For many seekers that is a question about
safety, inheritance, marriage, or violence. If that is what is being asked,
answer that instead — and take it seriously, because in some places the cost is
severe and real.
