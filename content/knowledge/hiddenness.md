# If God is real, why isn't he obvious?

## The objection

If God wants a relationship with people, he could make his existence
unmistakable — and does not. Many sincere people look and find nothing. That
asymmetry is a real argument, not an excuse, and it deserves to be treated as
one.

Sometimes the words are philosophical and the meaning is personal: _I prayed
and nothing happened._ Listen for which is being said.

## Responses Christians have offered

**Overwhelming evidence would change the relationship.** A God whose existence
was as undeniable as the sun would be related to as a fact rather than trusted
as a person. Coercion and love are hard to combine — though this cuts less far
than apologists often claim, since plenty of people believe firmly and still
love freely.

**The claim is that he has been obvious, in a particular place.** Christianity
does not claim a God who stayed hidden; it claims one who showed up as a
first-century labourer. The complaint then becomes that the revelation is not
distributed evenly, which is a different and better objection.

**Hiddenness is a biblical theme, not an embarrassment.** "Truly you are a God
who hides himself" (Isaiah 45:15). "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"
is quoted by Jesus at the moment it would be least convenient. Scripture
contains the complaint rather than suppressing it.

**Some looking is not neutral.** Held gently, and never as an accusation: what
we want to be true shapes what we find. This applies to believers at least as
much, and saying so to a seeker almost always lands as a personal attack. Most
volunteers should leave it alone.

## Honest limits

This objection is not fully answered. Many thoughtful Christians hold it as an
open difficulty rather than a solved problem. Saying so is more persuasive than
a confident answer that does not survive follow-up questions, and it treats the
person as someone capable of handling a real conversation.

## What is often more useful

Ask what they were looking for and what they expected to find. People asking
this have frequently had one specific experience — an unanswered prayer during
a crisis — and generalised from it. That specific thing is usually the
conversation worth having.
